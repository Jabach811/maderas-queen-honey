Good people, good honey

We’re a family from Madera. We started keeping bees in 2020, and it turned into all of this — apiaries across the Central Valley, a table at the local markets, and jars in more kitchens than we ever expected.

Our bees do double duty. Besides making honey, they pollinate family farms around the Valley, which means healthier crops for our neighbors and better forage for the hives. Keeping bees well is keeping the land well — we don’t see a difference between the two.

Every jar is raw and unfiltered. That’s not a marketing line; it’s just how honey should be.

Common questions

Where does the honey come from?
Our own hives, in apiaries around Madera County and the Central Valley. We harvest, jar, and label it ourselves.
Is it really raw?
Yes. We don’t heat it or fine-filter it, so the natural enzymes and pollen stay in the jar.
My honey turned grainy — is it bad?
No, that’s crystallization, and it’s a sign of real raw honey. Set the jar in warm water and it turns liquid again. Store it in a cool, dry spot with the lid closed.
Can I cook with it?
Absolutely — baking, sauces, dressings, tea. Just know that heat softens the raw benefits, so save a little for drizzling too.
